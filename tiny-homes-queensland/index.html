Living closer together

Multigenerational living is becoming a practical choice for more families.

Families are finding new ways to live closer together—while creating enough independence, privacy and flexibility for everyone.

For some, it is about supporting ageing parents. For others, it is about helping adult children establish themselves, sharing everyday costs or creating more time together as a family.

02

Why families are exploring it

More affordable living

Sharing household expenses such as mortgages, utilities and maintenance can make a meaningful difference to family budgets.

Support that is close by

Families can be closer to ageing parents while preserving the independence and dignity of their own space.

Practical family help

Living nearby can make it easier to share childcare, provide support and spend more time with the people who matter.

Privacy and connection

Self-contained living spaces can offer a thoughtful balance between independence and being part of the family home.

Making better use of home

A secondary dwelling can help a property evolve with your family’s needs instead of forcing a move.

04

Common questions

What is a secondary dwelling?

It is an additional, self-contained living space associated with a property. The relevant planning, building and servicing pathway depends on the property and proposal.

Can a tiny home be used as a secondary dwelling?

A tiny home may be considered as part of a secondary-dwelling proposal, but requirements depend on the individual property, proposal and intended use.

Do I need planning approval?

It depends on the property and proposal. Planning, building, plumbing and local requirements may apply, so obtain property-specific guidance before committing.

Where can I learn more about tiny homes in Queensland?

Read our Tiny Homes in Queensland guide, or refer to the Queensland Planning tiny homes guidance.
